# CONSTRUCTION SAFETY – IDENTIFYING RISKS BEFORE ACCIDENTS HAPPEN

Small signs in the field can be early warnings of a major incident

8/24/2026

**Identify risks before responding to incidents**

Construction safety is not only about responding to incidents. More importantly, it is about identifying risks before they become incidents.

Site conditions change continuously through each stage of construction. People, equipment, materials and work areas frequently move. A location that is safe in the morning may present new risks in the afternoon as the layout, access routes or operating range of machinery changes.

**Small signs that can easily be missed**

A PPE violation.
An unsafe behaviour.
A person entering a restricted area.
An unusual situation in the field.

These small signs may appear every day and can easily be overlooked. A single event may not lead to an accident, but repeated events can indicate an underlying risk in a procedure, work arrangement or site condition.

**Why manual supervision cannot cover the entire site**

Safety officers play an irreplaceable role in assessing conditions, guiding workers and deciding how to respond. However, they cannot be present in every location or continuously observe every activity at the same time.

An unsafe behaviour may last only a few seconds. A person may enter the operating zone of equipment immediately after a supervisor has moved away. A PPE violation may occur outside the direct line of sight. When a site involves multiple levels, work zones or contractors, the observation challenge becomes even greater.

Cameras can extend visibility, but relying on people alone to monitor screens can still create information overload. No one can maintain the same level of concentration for many hours and reliably identify every unusual situation.

**AI supports continuous site observation**

AI Video Analytics can support safety teams by continuously observing site conditions, detecting risks early and providing data for proactive safety improvement.

Instead of requiring safety officers to watch multiple screens continuously, a system can support the identification of events that deserve attention, such as PPE violations, entry into restricted areas or unusual situations within a monitored zone.

An alert helps the safety team focus on the relevant location and moment. Related video or images can also provide information for verifying the event and supporting internal discussion, training or review.

**From individual detections to visible patterns**

The value of data extends beyond individual alerts. When events are aggregated over time, an organisation can identify which areas repeatedly generate violations, which risks occur most often and when additional supervision may be needed.

If the same unsafe behaviour repeatedly occurs in one area, the cause may relate to the site layout, warning signs, work procedures or coordination between teams. Data allows safety discussions to be based on recorded situations rather than perception alone.

**People remain at the centre of safety**

AI does not make decisions on behalf of safety officers, nor does it remove the responsibilities of management, contractors or workers. Assessing risk severity, verifying context and selecting the appropriate action still require human experience and accountability.

Technology acts as an additional observation layer: it can extend monitoring coverage, reduce the risk of missed events and provide more information for decision-making.

**A safer construction site begins with earlier visibility**

Accidents are often perceived as unexpected events. Yet many incidents may be preceded by small signs: a recurring violation, repeated entry into a restricted area or an unusual condition that has not been addressed.

Recognising these signs earlier gives the safety team more time to investigate, intervene and prevent harm before consequences occur.

*Detect earlier. Prevent better. Build a safer site.*
