# The Workforce Challenge in the Integrated Security Industry

Why workforce development is becoming a strategic capability in the Integrated Security industry.

5/31/2026

The shortage of skilled professionals has become one of the most significant challenges facing the Integrated Security industry today.

This is not merely a recruitment issue. It is increasingly becoming a capability challenge that affects the growth, innovation, and long-term sustainability of organizations across the industry.

According to a global industry survey conducted by asmag, more than 80% of organizations reported workforce-related challenges. Over 75% identified skills gaps and salary expectations as major obstacles to recruitment, while more than 75% confirmed that workforce shortages have directly impacted business operations and growth.

These figures highlight a reality that many organizations already understand: finding qualified professionals has become increasingly difficult, especially in fields that require both technical expertise and practical implementation experience.

## The Growing Skills Gap

Modern Integrated Security systems are no longer limited to surveillance cameras and access control solutions.

Today's engineers and system specialists are expected to understand multiple disciplines, including:

Information Technology infrastructure
Communication networks
Cybersecurity
Cloud technologies
Artificial Intelligence
Database systems
Integration platforms and protocols

More importantly, they must understand how these technologies work together within real operational environments.

As technology continues to evolve at an unprecedented pace, workforce development struggles to keep up. Industry requirements are becoming increasingly complex, while the number of professionals with sufficient hands-on experience remains limited.

## Experience Cannot Be Learned from Theory Alone

System integration is a profession that is difficult to master through classroom education alone.

Technical knowledge can be taught through textbooks, courses, and certifications. However, real integration capability is often developed through practical experience.

It is built through:

High-pressure project deployments
Complex troubleshooting situations
Mission-critical systems that cannot afford downtime
Real-world responsibilities and decision-making

These experiences shape professionals who are capable of transforming technology into reliable operational systems.

## Building Talent from Within

As workforce shortages continue, many organizations are changing their approach.

Rather than relying solely on the labor market to provide fully qualified professionals, companies are increasingly investing in:

Internal training programs
Apprenticeship initiatives
Mentorship and coaching
Continuous professional development

The reality is that highly capable integration professionals are rarely created through a single training course. They are developed over years of learning, project exposure, and practical experience.

In a rapidly evolving industry, workforce development is no longer solely an HR responsibility. It has become a strategic business capability.

## Technology Needs People

Artificial Intelligence and automation are transforming the way organizations operate.

However, technology alone cannot replace professionals who understand how to connect systems, processes, and operational requirements into a functioning ecosystem.

A demonstration can be built within days.

An AI model can be trained within weeks.

Yet deploying, integrating, and maintaining a reliable operational system over many years requires a different level of expertise.

That is where practical system integration experience continues to create value.

## Looking Ahead

Over the next decade, one of the most important competitive advantages for organizations in the Integrated Security industry may not be the technologies they own.

Instead, it may be their ability to attract, develop, and retain skilled professionals who can successfully transform technology into real-world operational outcomes.

Technology creates possibilities.

People transform those possibilities into value.

Source: asmag Global Industry Survey, “The Manpower Issue: Security Players Are Broadly Affected, but Remedy Is in the Making” (2026).
