# NEAR MISS – NO ACCIDENT TODAY. BUT WHAT ABOUT TOMORROW?

A Near Miss is not just a lucky escape. It is a warning signal that can help identify risks before they become accidents.

9/22/2026

**NEAR MISS – NO ACCIDENT TODAY. BUT WHAT ABOUT TOMORROW?**

An object falls from above but narrowly misses a worker.

A forklift almost collides with someone.

Two vehicles nearly crash at an intersection.

No one is injured. Nothing is damaged. No accident occurs.

**“That was lucky. Nothing happened.”**

And everyone moves on.

But this mindset can lead to one of the most serious mistakes in safety management: **a Near Miss is ignored simply because there was no consequence.**

**A NEAR MISS DOES NOT MEAN WE ARE SAFE**

A Near Miss is often treated as a lucky escape.

There is no injury, no damage, and no serious incident to investigate.

As a result, Near Misses can easily go **unreported, unanalyzed, and ultimately unresolved.**

But a Near Miss is not proof that the environment or system is safe.

Instead, it may indicate that **the conditions for an accident were already present – the consequence simply did not occur this time.**

A Near Miss ignored today may happen again tomorrow.

A second time. A tenth time. A hundredth time.

**Until one day, the Near Miss becomes an Accident.**

**FROM WORKPLACE SAFETY...**

In the workplace, Near Misses can result from many different factors:

Unsafe behaviors; missing or improperly used PPE; dangerous interactions between people and vehicles; entry into restricted areas; falling-object risks; and hazardous workplace conditions.

The objective should not be limited to identifying what happened after an accident.

**We also need to recognize the behaviors and conditions that could create a Near Miss before an accident occurs.**

Technology today can support the detection, recording, and analysis of these warning signals more systematically, particularly across large or continuously operating environments.

**...TO ROAD SAFETY**

The same principle can be applied to Road Safety.

Two vehicles nearly collide at an intersection but brake in time.

A vehicle makes a dangerous lane change without causing a crash.

A pedestrian and a vehicle pass through the same conflict point with only a small distance or time gap between them.

There may be no accident, but **a traffic conflict has already occurred.**

Technology can help identify risky driving behaviors, traffic conflict points, unsafe traffic conditions, deficiencies in traffic organization, and infrastructure issues that may increase risk.

Instead of analyzing only locations **where accidents have already happened**, data can also help us identify locations **where accidents may be more likely to occur.**

**A DIFFERENT QUESTION ABOUT SAFETY**

We often ask:

**“How many accidents have occurred?”**

It is an important question. But perhaps we should also ask:

**“Before an accident happened, how many times did it almost happen – and we ignored the warning?”**

When these warning signals are detected and recorded, they can become valuable data for understanding root causes, identifying risk patterns, and taking corrective action.

**Detect → Record → Analyze → Improve → Prevent**

This is also where technology can help shift safety management from **reacting after incidents** toward **proactively identifying and preventing risks.**

**DON’T IGNORE A NEAR MISS TODAY, ONLY TO INVESTIGATE AN ACCIDENT TOMORROW.**
