# PROACTIVE SAFETY MANAGEMENT

Using AI and data to identify risk indicators before incidents occur

8/23/2026

**From limited observation to proactive management**

In modern manufacturing and operating environments, one of the greatest safety challenges is that people cannot observe every location, every moment and every action.

An unsafe behaviour may last only a few seconds. A hazardous condition may persist for a long time without being detected during periodic inspections. This is where AI and data can support organisations in moving towards proactive safety management.

**Safety is more than compliance**

An organisation may have comprehensive procedures, training programmes and personal protective equipment in place. Yet a gap can still emerge between procedures and reality: workers may use PPE incorrectly, enter restricted areas, approach machinery at an unsafe distance or perform actions that could lead to an incident.

Safety management should not rely only on the number of accidents, injuries or lost working days. These are lagging indicators that tell us what has already happened.

**Leading indicators that deserve attention**

The frequency of PPE violations.
The number of entries into hazardous areas.
The recurrence of unsafe behaviours.
Locations or times where risks repeatedly arise.
The time taken to respond to and resolve alerts.

**AI as a continuous safety observation layer**

Through existing camera systems, AI can support the real-time identification of unsafe behaviours and conditions. Instead of requiring safety officers to monitor dozens or hundreds of screens continuously, the system helps them focus on events that genuinely require attention.

AI does not replace safety officers. It provides an additional observation layer that helps detect risks faster, reduces dependence on manual inspection and creates objective data for analysis.

**From alerts to improvement**

The value of AI is not limited to generating alerts. When events are aggregated and analysed, an organisation can identify systemic issues.

If the same violation repeatedly appears in a particular area, shift or work group, the cause may extend beyond individual awareness. The organisation may need to review its procedures, training, work arrangements and operating conditions.

Safety data then becomes a basis for identifying root causes and improving the management system.
